excerpt: 'The performance prediction of fuel rods within nuclear reactor cores serves as the foundation for fuel design evaluation. Traditional approaches involve the utilization of numerical algorithms such as finite differences to solve a series of partial differential equations. However, these numerical methods face challenges in terms of computational efficiency in scenarios such as core-level fuel rod performance assessment and high-fidelity calculation. This study proposes a hybrid Fourier neural Operator which consists of three LSTMs and an FNO-LSTM integrating physical information to predict the spatiotemporal distribution of fuel rod temperature and mechanic field. Gaussian random field combined with physical principles is used to generate a broad and physically consistent power history. The results show that the surrogate model has good performance, with the mean error of pellet temperature, plenum pressure, hoop stress, and hoop strain in the actual core dataset is 0.75 K, 0.03 MPa, 1.16 MPa, and 4.07e-5. Compared with machine learning algorithms that predict extreme values, it can predict more information with less data and has better accuracy and generalization. In terms of runtime, the surrogate model can achieve 1700 acceleration compared to the 1.5 dimension fuel performance code.'
